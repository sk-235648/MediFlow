# Routine Conditions, Outpatient Clinic & Self-Care Protocols

## 1. Uncomplicated Upper Respiratory Infection (Common Cold)
### Clinical Features
- Clear rhinorrhea, nasal congestion, mild scratchy sore throat, sneezing, low-grade temperature (< 38.0°C).
- Absence of high fever, dyspnea, wheezing, or toxic appearance.
- **Triage Level**: **ESI Level 5** (Non-Urgent / Self-Care).
- **Venue**: Home self-care or outpatient primary care if symptoms persist > 10 days.
- **Supportive Recommendations**:
  - Rest, oral hydration (warm fluids, broth).
  - Normal saline nasal spray or rinse.
  - Honey for cough relief (only in individuals > 1 year of age; never in infants under 1 due to infant botulism risk).
  - Humidified air.
- **Red Flag Return Warnings**: Sudden difficulty breathing, chest pain, high fever > 39.0°C unresponsive to antipyretics, or inability to tolerate liquids.

## 2. Acute Uncomplicated Gastroenteritis
### Clinical Features
- Mild to moderate non-bloody watery diarrhea, nausea, mild cramping relieved by defecation.
- Retaining liquids, normal urine output, no orthostatic dizziness or tachycardia.
- **Triage Level**: **ESI Level 4 or 5**.
- **Venue**: Home self-care with oral rehydration solution (ORS); primary care clinic if prolonged.
- **Red Flags Requiring Immediate Escalation**:
  - Inability to retain any liquids for > 12-24 hours.
  - Frank hematochezia (bloody stool) or coffee-ground / bloody emesis.
  - Severe localized peritoneal abdominal pain (rigid abdomen, rebound tenderness).
  - Signs of severe dehydration (anuria > 12 hours, tachycardia, postural syncope).

## 3. Minor Musculoskeletal Sprains / Strains
### Clinical Features
- Mild ankle inversion injury without inability to bear weight.
- Ottawa Ankle Rules check: If unable to bear weight for 4 steps OR bone tenderness at posterior edge of malleoli -> order ankle X-ray (**ESI Level 4**). If able to bear weight and no bony tenderness -> **ESI Level 5** (RICE protocol: Rest, Ice, Compression, Elevation).
- **Safety Directive**: Do not diagnose fractures or prescribe controlled narcotics; direct to outpatient urgent care or primary physician if swelling or inability to walk persists.
