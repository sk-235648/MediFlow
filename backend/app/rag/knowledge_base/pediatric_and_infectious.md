# Pediatric Emergencies, Sepsis & Infectious Disease Protocols

## 1. Sepsis & Systemic Toxicity
### Pediatric & Adult Sepsis Screening
- Elevated temperature (> 38.5°C or 101.3°F) OR Hypothermia (< 36.0°C or 96.8°F).
- Tachycardia out of proportion to fever (e.g. HR > 110 bpm resting in adult).
- Tachypnea (respiratory rate > 22 bpm in adult).
- Altered mental status, acute confusion, or extreme lethargy.
- Mottled extremities, delayed capillary refill (> 3 seconds), cool periphery.
- Non-blanching petechial or purpuric rash (critical sign of meningococcal septicemia).
- **Triage Level**: **ESI Level 2** (Immediate broad-spectrum IV antibiotics, blood cultures, fluid resuscitation).

## 2. Pediatric Specific Red Flags (Pediatric Assessment Triangle - PAT)
- **Appearance**: Poor muscle tone, weak or high-pitched cry, unresponsiveness, lack of eye contact.
- **Work of Breathing**: Grunting, nasal flaring, head bobbing, severe intercostal or subcostal retractions.
- **Circulation to Skin**: Pallor, cyanosis, mottling.
- **Infant Age Red Flag**: Any infant under 28 days of age with temperature >= 38.0°C (100.4°F) is considered a neonatal emergency requiring full septic workup. **ESI Level 2**.

## 3. Acute Severe Dehydration
- Sunken fontanelle (in infants), sunken eyes, dry mucous membranes, no tears when crying, no urination for > 8-12 hours, tachycardia, delayed capillary refill.
- Triage Level: **ESI Level 2 or 3**.
