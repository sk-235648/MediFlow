# Respiratory Emergencies & Acute Dyspnea Protocol

## 1. Acute Respiratory Distress & Failure
### Signs of Severe Respiratory Distress
- Inability to speak in full sentences (broken speech, 1-2 words between breaths).
- Accessory muscle use: Sternocleidomastoid retractions, intercostal indrawing, tripod positioning.
- Stridor (audible high-pitched inspiratory wheeze indicating upper airway obstruction).
- Cyanosis (bluish tint on lips, tongue, or fingertips).
- SpO2 < 90% on room air, or acute drop > 5% from patient baseline.
- **Triage Level**: **ESI Level 1 (if agonal/exhaustion)** or **ESI Level 2 (severe distress)**.
- **Action**: Immediate high-flow oxygen, nebulized bronchodilators if indicated, emergency airway readiness.

## 2. Acute Asthma / COPD Exacerbation
### Severity Stratification
- **Life-Threatening (ESI Level 1)**: Silent chest (absence of wheezing due to severe air trapping and fatigue), altered mentation, bradycardia, exhaustion.
- **Severe (ESI Level 2)**: Peak expiratory flow < 50% predicted, SpO2 < 92%, tachypnea > 28 bpm, diaphoresis.
- **Moderate (ESI Level 3)**: Wheezing present, able to speak sentences with mild pause, SpO2 92-95%, tachycardia 100-115 bpm.
- **Mild (ESI Level 4/5)**: Mild wheezing/cough, normal speech, SpO2 > 96%, responds promptly to home inhaler.

## 3. Pulmonary Embolism (PE)
### Presentation & Wells Criteria Red Flags
- Sudden unexplained onset of dyspnea and pleuritic chest pain.
- Unilateral calf swelling, erythema, tenderness (deep vein thrombosis sign).
- Tachycardia (HR > 100 bpm) without other explanation.
- Hemoptysis (coughing up blood).
- Risk factors: Recent surgery, immobilization, active malignancy, oral contraceptive use, prolonged travel.
- **Triage Level**: **ESI Level 2**.
- **Action**: Urgent CTA chest or V/Q scan, D-dimer, oxygen therapy.

## 4. Anaphylaxis (Airway Compromise)
- Rapid onset of urticaria (hives), pruritus, angioedema (swelling of tongue/throat/lips).
- Accompanied by respiratory compromise (wheezing, stridor) OR hypotension (dizziness, collapse).
- **Triage Level**: **ESI Level 1 or 2**.
- **First-line Action**: Immediate Intramuscular Epinephrine (0.3 - 0.5 mg 1:1000 in anterolateral thigh); immediate emergency room dispatch.
