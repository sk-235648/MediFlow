# Neurological Emergencies & Acute Stroke Protocol

## 1. Acute Ischemic / Hemorrhagic Stroke
### BE-FAST Screening Tool
- **B - Balance**: Sudden loss of balance, ataxia, vertigo, or coordination failure.
- **E - Eyes**: Sudden loss of vision in one or both eyes, diplopia (double vision), or visual field cut.
- **F - Face**: Facial asymmetry, unilateral facial droop, uneven smile.
- **A - Arms**: Unilateral arm weakness or drift, heaviness, numbness.
- **S - Speech**: Slurred speech (dysarthria), expressive or receptive aphasia, garbled words.
- **T - Time**: Time of Last Known Well (LKW) is paramount. Thrombolysis (tPA/TNK) window is within 4.5 hours; endovascular thrombectomy window up to 24 hours in selected large vessel occlusions.
- **Triage Level**: **ESI Level 2** (Code Stroke / Rapid Emergency Department Activation).
- **Timeframe**: Immediate EMS transfer. Patient must NOT eat or drink due to aspiration risk.

## 2. Acute "Thunderclap" Headache / Subarachnoid Hemorrhage
### Red Flags (SNOOP4 Criteria)
- Abrupt onset reaching maximal agonizing intensity within seconds to 1 minute ("worst headache of life").
- Associated neck stiffness (meningismus), photophobia, vomiting, syncope, or seizure.
- Triage Level: **ESI Level 2**.
- Immediate non-contrast brain CT scan and possible lumbar puncture.

## 3. Status Epilepticus & Seizures
- Active continuous seizure activity lasting > 5 minutes, or 2+ sequential seizures without return to baseline consciousness.
- **Triage Level**: **ESI Level 1**.
- Single uncomplicated post-ictal seizure in known epileptic patient returning to baseline: **ESI Level 3**.
- First-time new seizure in adult: **ESI Level 2**.

## 4. Acute Altered Mental Status / Delirium
- Sudden confusion, disorientation, lethargy, or failure to recognize familiar persons.
- Requires immediate check of point-of-care fingerstick blood glucose (rule out hypoglycemia < 60 mg/dL).
- Triage Level: **ESI Level 2**.
